DES221 / PERSONAL DESIGN PRACTICE

DESIGN LOG_001 ✳

Building my
own identity.

How I turned my interest in tattoo art into a flexible personal visual identity — from early linework and typography sketches to a recognisable design system.

PROJECTPersonal Visual Identity
PRACTICEDesign exploration & reflection
CONTEXTDES221 · Personal Design Practice
01 / THE QUESTION

How do I make my work feel like me?

My interest in tattoo design has always been drawn to flowing lines, clean compositions and expressive detail. But individual drawings did not automatically form a clear design identity. I wanted to develop a consistent way to present my practice — one that felt personal without restricting me to tattoo art alone.

“Minimal. Flowing. Expressive.”
02 / RESEARCH & DIRECTION

Finding the visual language.

I explored delicate linework, expressive serif typography, and a controlled colour palette. Black and off-white provide the foundation; deep red adds personality. The challenge was to create a system that could extend beyond a single drawing or medium.

SOURCE: ORIGINAL DES221 PROCESS DOCUMENTATION · MOODBOARD

03 / EXPERIMENT & ITERATION

From wordmark to expression.

I started with an elegant serif typeface and adjusted its proportions. I added tattoo-inspired curves around the letters, but some versions became too crowded. I then simplified the linework, refined the connections and introduced small star-shaped accents to retain personality without sacrificing readability.

SOURCE: ORIGINAL DES221 PROCESS DOCUMENTATION · LOGO EXPLORATION

04 / BUILDING A SYSTEM

Details that work together.

I developed a family of related forms: starbursts, flowing curves, sharp connections and thorn-like elements. These can be rearranged across layouts while sharing a recognisable visual character.

SOURCE: ORIGINAL DES221 PROCESS DOCUMENTATION · ELEMENT EXPLORATION

05 / APPLICATION & REFLECTION

More than a single style.

I tested the visual language across website, mobile, business card and social mockups. This made me think beyond individual artworks and consider how someone experiences my practice as a whole. I realised that my identity needs to communicate an approach to design, not just one artistic interest.

SOURCE: ORIGINAL DES221 PROCESS DOCUMENTATION · BRAND APPLICATION

“A personal identity should reflect what I care about now, while leaving space for who I might become.”
06 / WHAT'S NEXT

A practice that keeps growing.

This portfolio reimagines my existing brand as a Tattoo Cyber Y2K digital environment while retaining the original black, off-white and deep-red identity. The metallic details, cursor interactions and digital tabby cat are new web-layer explorations, not claims about my earlier logo research.

My next steps are to keep adding original work, document design decisions more consistently, and update this identity as my creative practice develops.
